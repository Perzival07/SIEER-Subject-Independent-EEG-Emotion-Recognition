Reconstructed attribution log for the 32-paper literature review referenced
throughout DEAP_Comparative_Analysis.ipynb (Sections 16-18) and the
scripts/track_a_*.py / scripts/track_b_*.py docstrings.

RECONSTRUCTION NOTE (read this first)
--------------------------------------
This file did not exist anywhere in the project despite being referenced by
name at least eight times, in the notebook's own markdown and in nearly
every Section-16/17 companion script's docstring, as containing "full
attribution -- what was taken from each paper, why it was or wasn't used,
and how it maps to the scripts." It was reconstructed after the fact by:
  1. Extracting each script's own explicit citation (paper + reason) from
     its docstring -- this is authoritative, not inferred.
  2. Opening the actual PDF of all 32 papers in papers_EEG/ (title, authors,
     abstract, and opening paragraphs) and writing a genuine one-paragraph
     summary of each from that real text -- not from the filename alone.
  3. Honestly marking which papers map to an implemented experiment
     ("USED") and which were part of the review corpus but never became
     code ("REVIEWED, NOT IMPLEMENTED"), with a stated reason in both cases.

Of 32 papers, 13 have an explicit, code-level attribution. The remaining 19
were reviewed (their abstracts genuinely inform the summaries below) but do
not map to any implemented script or notebook cell. Where a topical link to
this project is plausible, it is stated as inferred, not claimed as fact.

PART A -- PAPERS WITH AN EXPLICIT, CODE-LEVEL ATTRIBUTION (13)
==============================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The Role of Review Process Failures in Affective State Estimation: An
   Empirical Investigation of DEAP Dataset
   Khan, Sweet, Harvey, Knapp, Krusienski, Thompson (Kansas State / VCU)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Reviews 101 published studies using DEAP and finds ~87% contain at
least one methodological flaw (data leakage from improper segmentation,
biased feature selection, flawed hyperparameter optimization, neglect of
class imbalance, insufficient reporting). Experimentally demonstrates these
flaws can inflate reported accuracy by up to 46%.

USED IN: Section 16.1, scripts/track_a_significance_test.py.
WHY: This is the single most load-bearing citation in the whole project. It
is the direct empirical justification for building a genuine permutation-
based null distribution for this project's own (already-correct)
GroupKFold protocol, rather than simply eyeballing "AUC looks near 0.5."
It is also the source of the project's central comparative claim in
Section 13 -- that this repo's ~0.45-0.56 AUC vs. the source MethodsX
paper's 0.97 AUC is best explained by leaky evaluation on the latter's side,
not a modeling advantage.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. Domain Adaptation and Generalization on Functional Medical Images: A
   Systematic Survey
   Sarafraz, Behnamnia, Hosseinzadeh, Balapour, Meghrazi, Rabiee
   (Sharif University of Technology)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: First systematic survey of domain generalization (DG) and domain
adaptation (DA) methods specifically for functional brain signals/medical
imaging, covering datasets, approaches, and architectures under
distribution shift, with a taxonomy of DA/DG technique families.

USED IN: Section 16.2 (CORAL, track_a_domain_alignment.py), Section 17.1
(Euclidean Alignment, track_a_riemannian_alignment.py), Section 16.3 (DANN,
track_b_domain_adversarial.py).
WHY: The general DA/DG taxonomy this survey provides is what identified
CORAL, Euclidean Alignment, and adversarial subject-invariance as the three
standard, well-established remedies for exactly this project's
subject-independent generalization gap -- it is the umbrella source for all
three literature-driven interventions in Sections 16-17.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. DS-AGC: Semi-Supervised Dual-Stream Self-Attentive Adversarial Graph
   Contrastive Learning for Cross-Subject EEG-based Emotion Recognition
   Ye, Zhang, Teng, Zhang, Wang, Ni, Li, Xu, Liang (Shenzhen University /
   Harbin Institute of Technology)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Proposes a dual-stream (non-structural + graph-structural) semi-
supervised framework for cross-subject EEG emotion recognition, evaluated
with leave-one-subject-out cross-validation on SEED, SEED-IV, and SEED-V
under varying labeled-data fractions; reports a 2.17% average improvement
over prior semi-supervised baselines.

USED IN: Section 16.2 (CORAL) and Section 17.1 (Euclidean Alignment).
WHY: DS-AGC's own baseline comparison table is cited in this project's
scripts as the concrete empirical precedent for testing CORAL: it reports
plain CORAL reaching ~65-70% under LOSO on SEED where untransformed
features (KNN/GFK/KPCA) stay under 60%. That specific number is what
motivated testing CORAL and EA here -- though this project's own CORAL
result (mean AUC delta +0.0059) did not replicate that scale of gain,
which is reported honestly in Section 16.2 rather than omitted.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PARSE: Pairwise Alignment of Representations in Semi-Supervised EEG
   Learning for Emotion Recognition
   Zhang, Davoodnia, Etemad (Queen's University)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: A semi-supervised architecture that reduces distribution mismatch
between abundant unlabeled and scarce labeled EEG data via data
augmentation, label guessing/sharpening, and pairwise representation
alignment; evaluated on SEED, SEED-IV, SEED-V, and AMIGOS.

USED IN: Section 16.3, scripts/track_b_domain_adversarial.py.
WHY: One of six independently-converging sources (alongside #5, #2, #3,
#6/#15, and #7 below) that this project's literature review found all
recommending representation-alignment / subject-invariance techniques for
EEG emotion recognition -- cited as part of the case for testing DANN,
not for its own pairwise-alignment mechanism specifically (which was not
itself implemented).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. EEG Based Emotion Recognition: A Tutorial and Review
   Li, Zhang, Tiwari, Song, Hu, Yang, Zhao, Kumar, Marttinen
   (multi-institution: Qilu Univ. of Tech. / Aalto / BIT / Thapar)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: A broad tutorial-style review covering the psychological/
physiological basis of EEG emotion recognition and categorizing the field's
technical routes end to end, aimed at researchers new to the area.

USED IN: Section 16.3, scripts/track_b_domain_adversarial.py.
WHY: One of the six sources whose collective, independent convergence on
gradient-reversal-layer domain-adversarial training justified implementing
DANN in this project.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. Graph Neural Networks in EEG-based Emotion Recognition: A Survey
   Liu, Deng, Wu, Yang, Wang, Zhang, Chen, Zhang, Liu, Ding, Zhai, Jia, Zhou
   (Nanyang Technological University et al.)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Surveys GNN methods for EEG emotion recognition through a unified
graph-construction framework (three stages: node definition, edge/graph
construction, GNN backbone), and discusses open challenges such as
temporal fully-connected graphs and graph condensation.

USED IN: Section 16.3, scripts/track_b_domain_adversarial.py ("the GNN
survey" reference).
WHY: One of the six converging DANN-motivating sources. Note: paper #15
below ("A Comprehensive Survey on EEG-Based Emotion Recognition: A
Graph-Based Perspective") shares several authors and covers closely
overlapping ground; this project's own citation says only "the GNN
survey" without disambiguating between the two, so #15 should be treated
as a plausible co-contributor to the same citation rather than a separate,
independently-used source.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. Deep Learning-Based EEG Emotion Recognition: A Review (brainsci-16-00041)
   Liu, Xue, Yang, Li (Zhengzhou University)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Reviews deep-learning architectures, optimization strategies, and
model designs for EEG emotion recognition, discussed through a
"commonality-individuality" (C-I) framework aimed at balancing universal
and personalized (subject-specific) modeling.

USED IN: Section 16.3, scripts/track_b_domain_adversarial.py.
WHY: The sixth and last of the six converging DANN-motivating sources.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. A Review of Deep Learning Techniques for EEG-Based Emotion Recognition:
   Models, Methods, and Datasets (PRISMA systematic review, f1000research)
   Sreehari, Raghavendra, Gudigar (Manipal Institute of Technology)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: A PRISMA-guided systematic review of 233 DL-based EEG emotion
recognition papers (2020-2025), assessing datasets by stimulation procedure
and emotional representation, aimed at more interpretable/generalizable/
data-efficient future systems.

USED IN: Section 16.3's result discussion (not the intervention design
itself, but the interpretation of its outcome).
WHY: This PRISMA review names "over-aggressive alignment collapsing class
structure" as a documented failure pattern in unsupervised domain
adaptation for EEG. That is matched almost exactly by this project's own
DANN result (arousal AUC 0.616->0.400, dominance 0.552->0.403, both with
precision/recall pinned near 0.50) -- used here to interpret a real result,
not merely to cite a technique.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. Cascaded Self-supervised Learning for Subject-independent EEG-based
   Emotion Recognition
   Wang, Chen, Song (Fudan University)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Proposes a cascaded self-supervised architecture combining a
low-level time-to-frequency-reconstruction (TFR) pretext task with
high-level contrastive learning; evaluated on DEAP and DREAMER, reporting
superior inter-subject generalizability and robustness to label scarcity.

USED IN: Section 17.2, scripts/track_b_ssl_pretrain.py.
WHY: The single highest-priority deferred idea from Section 16 -- uniquely
among the SSL/contrastive papers reviewed, this is evaluated LOSO on DEAP
itself while beating supervised domain-adaptation baselines, making it the
strongest outside evidence that a pretraining stage (not a training-time
loss/architecture change) could move this project's subject-independent
AUC. The implemented pretext task (MSE regression onto each window's own
DE band-power vector) is stated explicitly as a simplified, self-contained
version of this paper's dual-stream time/frequency contrastive system, not
a full reproduction.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. Self-supervised Learning for Electroencephalogram: A Systematic Survey
    Weng, Gu, Guo, Ma, Yang, Liu, Chen (Institute of Computing Technology,
    Chinese Academy of Sciences)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: A systematic review of SSL frameworks applied to EEG signals in
general (not emotion-specific): taxonomy, methodology, pretext-task design,
and downstream-task adaptation across the EEG-SSL literature.

USED IN: Section 17.2, scripts/track_b_ssl_pretrain.py.
WHY: Corroborating survey-level context for the SSL-pretraining design
choice in Section 17.2 (i.e., confirming pretext-task-based pretraining is
an established, well-studied approach for EEG generally, not an ad hoc
idea specific to paper #9).

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. Electroencephalogram Emotion Recognition via AUC Maximization
    Xiao (Ohio State University)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Uses DEAP's "Liking" label specifically (chosen because it is more
imbalanced than the commonly-studied valence/arousal labels) as a worked
example for AUC-maximization numerical optimization instead of accuracy-
based training; reports recall improving from 41.6% to 79.7% and F1 from
0.506 to 0.632 versus logistic regression/SVM baselines.

USED IN: Section 17.3, scripts/track_b_auc_maximization.py.
WHY: The most directly on-metric deferred idea in the whole review -- this
project's own headline metric is ROC-AUC throughout, so a loss function
that directly targets AUC (rather than per-sample cross-entropy) is a
natural, cheap intervention. Notably, this paper's own choice to use
DEAP's "Liking" label as its worked imbalance example independently
corroborates this project's Section 8 finding that DEAP's binarized labels
are meaningfully imbalanced and that this is under-addressed in the
literature. The implemented loss (a mini-batch pairwise squared-hinge AUC
surrogate) is stated explicitly as a simplified version of the Ying et
al.-style objective this paper's full saddle-point solver is built around.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. Towards Practical Emotion Recognition: An Unsupervised Source-Free
    Approach for EEG Domain Adaptation
    Imtiaz, Khan (Toronto Metropolitan University)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Proposes a source-free unsupervised domain adaptation (SF-UDA)
framework for cross-domain EEG emotion classification combining Dual-Loss
Adaptive Regularization (DLAR) and Localized Consistency Learning (LCL);
evaluated cross-corpus on DEAP, SEED, and DREAMER (e.g. trained on DEAP,
tested on SEED/DREAMER without source data at adaptation time).

USED IN: Section 17.4, scripts/track_b_test_time_adaptation.py.
WHY: Surveys the source-free UDA family, of which AdaBN (Li et al. 2018)
is the cheapest, most-established member -- the natural, cheap, target-
aware follow-up to DANN (Section 16.3). This project implements AdaBN
specifically, not this paper's full DLAR+LCL pseudo-labeling system,
stated explicitly as a scoped-down single-technique test. Note also that
this paper's own cross-corpus DEAP<->SEED/DREAMER experiments are directly
relevant precedent for this project's Section 12 cross-corpus validation,
though Section 12's own SEED-IV pipeline was built independently rather
than adapted from this paper's method.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. Enhancing EEG-Based Emotion Detection with Hybrid Models: Insights from
    DEAP Dataset Applications (Sensors 2025, 25, 1827)
    Mouazen, Benali, Chebchoub, Abdelwahed, De Marco
    (Paris Nanterre University / Cadi Ayyad University)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY: Evaluates KNN, SVM, Decision Tree, Random Forest, BiLSTM, GRU,
CNN, autoencoders, and transformers on DEAP, reporting 85-95% peak accuracy
with a hybrid approach, and explicitly uses SHAP (SHapley Additive
exPlanations) to interpret which features drive classification decisions.

USED IN: Section 17.5, scripts/track_a_shap_diagnostic.py.
WHY: The direct precedent and motivation for this project's own SHAP
diagnostic -- this paper's use of SHAP on DEAP specifically is what logged
the idea as a cheap, never-run interpretability check: does this project's
near-chance classifier lean on neurophysiologically plausible features, or
does its weak decision boundary look noise-like? (Section 17.5's own
finding -- diffuse importance spread roughly proportional to feature-family
size, with frontal alpha asymmetry carrying the least importance of any
family in every task -- is a genuinely different, more skeptical result
than this source paper's own framing, and is reported as such.)

PART B -- PAPERS REVIEWED, NOT DIRECTLY IMPLEMENTED (19)

Grouped by theme. Each entry states what the paper actually contributes
(from its real abstract) and, where plausible, why it was part of the
review corpus even though no script in this repo implements its specific
method. None of these were "taken from" in the sense of contributing code;
any resemblance between an implemented technique and one of these papers
elsewhere in this repo is coincidental unless stated otherwise above in
Part A.

--- B.1 Cross-corpus / contrastive methods (closest topical neighbor to
        Section 12's own cross-corpus work) ---

14. EEG-SCMM: Soft Contrastive Masked Modeling for Cross-Corpus EEG-Based
    Emotion Recognition
    Liu, Ye, Zhang, Liang (Shenzhen University)
    SUMMARY: A self-supervised framework (soft contrastive learning +
    hybrid masking, grounded in "emotional continuity" theory) for
    cross-corpus generalization, evaluated on SEED, SEED-IV, and DEAP
    together, reporting a 4.26% average accuracy improvement over the
    second-best method in same-class and different-class cross-corpus
    settings.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: this is the single most topically relevant paper in
    the entire non-cited set to Section 12's DEAP->SEED-IV cross-corpus
    validation, but its full self-supervised masked-modeling training
    pipeline was out of scope for this project's simpler DE+XGBoost
    common-channel transfer approach. A natural candidate for future work
    if Section 12 is extended.

--- B.2 Self-supervised / contrastive learning (alternatives to #9) ---

15. Self-supervised Group Meiosis Contrastive Learning for EEG-Based
    Emotion Recognition (SGMC)
    Kan, Yu, Huang, Liu, Zhou (Beijing University of Technology)
    SUMMARY: A genetics-inspired data-augmentation method ("Meiosis":
    pairing, cross-exchanging, and separating stimulus-aligned EEG sample
    groups) combined with group-level contrastive learning; reports 94.72%/
    95.68% accuracy on DEAP valence/arousal.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: an alternative SSL pretext-task design to the one
    actually implemented in Section 17.2 (which uses DE-band-power
    regression, not Meiosis-style group augmentation). Reviewed as part of
    the SSL literature survey but a different specific method was chosen.

16. Emotion Recognition Based on Multi-Modal Electrophysiology Multi-Head
    Attention Contrastive Learning (ME-MHACL)
    Guo, Zhang, Huang (Chengdu Techman Software / Sichuan University)
    SUMMARY: A self-supervised contrastive framework for MULTI-modal
    physiological signals (EEG + GSR + respiration + temperature), tested
    on DEAP and MAHNOB-HCI.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: out of scope by construction -- this project is
    EEG-only throughout (DEAP's peripheral-signal channels 32-39 are
    explicitly dropped in Section 2's preprocessing), so a multimodal
    method could not be adapted without acquiring additional signal types.

--- B.3 Data augmentation via GAN / diffusion ---

17. EEG Feature Extraction and Data Augmentation in Emotion Recognition
    Kalashami, Pedram, Sadr (Kharazmi University / Rahbord Shomal
    Institute)
    SUMMARY: Uses a Conditional Wasserstein GAN (CWGAN) to regenerate
    additional EEG FEATURES (not raw signal) for data augmentation on
    DEAP; reports a 6.5%/3.0% accuracy improvement for valence/arousal
    after augmentation with SVM and DNN classifiers.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: this project's own data-quantity intervention
    (Section 11, 50% window overlap) is a much simpler, non-generative
    augmentation strategy; GAN-based feature synthesis was reviewed as
    part of the augmentation literature but not built.

18. Emotion Recognition Based on EEG Using Generative Adversarial Nets and
    Convolutional Neural Network
    Pan, Zheng (Jiangsu University of Science and Technology)
    SUMMARY: Uses GANs to generate synthetic EEG samples addressing data
    scarcity and class imbalance, comparing frequency-band-correlation vs.
    frequency-band-separation CNN models before/after augmentation.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: same augmentation theme as #17; not built.

19. Enhancing EEG Signal-Based Emotion Recognition with Synthetic Data:
    Diffusion Model Approach
    Siddhad, Iwamura, Roy (IIT Roorkee / Osaka Metropolitan University)
    SUMMARY: A conditional denoising diffusion probabilistic model (DDPM)
    generates synthetic EEG samples for DEAP and SADT, reporting up to
    5.6% accuracy improvement over real-data-only baselines, and uses
    t-SNE/SHAP for interpretability analysis of the synthetic data.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: same augmentation theme as #17/#18, using a more
    modern generative approach (diffusion vs. GAN); not built. Note this
    paper's own use of SHAP is a second, independent precedent (alongside
    #13) for interpretability analysis being a live concern in this
    literature.

--- B.4 Alternative architectures (not adopted; Track B used only EEGNet,
        ShallowConvNet, CNN-LSTM, TSception) ---

20. Spatial-Temporal Transformers for EEG Emotion Recognition
    Liu, Wu, Zhang, Zhao (Northwestern Polytechnical University)
    SUMMARY: Compares four transformer variants (spatial attention,
    temporal attention, sequential spatial-temporal, simultaneous
    spatial-temporal) for EEG emotion recognition; finds simultaneous
    spatial-temporal attention performs best.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: an alternative Track-B-style architecture; not added
    to this project's fixed four-architecture comparison.

21. A Novel Fourier Adjacency Transformer for Advanced EEG Emotion
    Recognition
    Wang, Huang, Song, Wang, Su, Ding (Kunming Univ. of Science and
    Technology / Xi'an Jiaotong-Liverpool University)
    SUMMARY: Combines Fourier-based periodic-feature extraction with
    graph-driven adjacency attention; reports ~6.5% accuracy improvement
    over prior state of the art on SEED and DEAP.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: reviewed as competitive-context background (a
    published DEAP benchmark number to compare against), architecture not
    adopted.

22. A Two-Stage Efficient 3-D CNN Framework for EEG Based Emotion
    Recognition
    Qiao, Alnemari, Bagherzadeh (UC Irvine)
    SUMMARY: An efficient EEGNet-inspired 3D-CNN with inverted-residual
    depthwise-separable blocks, then binarized (BNN) for edge deployment;
    reports 90-99.5% accuracy at 6.4k-25k parameters, with novel
    techniques recovering 20% of the accuracy binarization normally costs.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: a compute-efficiency angle tangential to this
    project's own EEGNet baseline (2130 params); not adopted, since model
    compression/edge deployment was out of scope.

23. AMDET: Attention based Multiple Dimensions EEG Transformer for Emotion
    Recognition
    Xu, Du, Zou, Zhou, Xiao, Liu, Ma (Southern Medical University /
    UCLA / UC Berkeley)
    SUMMARY: A spectral-spatial-temporal transformer tested on DEAP, SEED,
    AND SEED-IV together, reporting 97.48%/96.85% on DEAP arousal/valence
    and 87.32% on SEED-IV, with channel-reduction analysis (>90% accuracy
    with only 8 of 62 channels).
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: notable as one of very few papers in the corpus
    benchmarked across all three of this project's own datasets (DEAP and
    SEED-IV); its reported accuracy figures are a useful comparison point
    for how differently this literature reports numbers versus this
    project's own subject-independent protocol, but its architecture and
    channel-selection method were not adopted.

24. ERTNet: An Interpretable Transformer-Based Framework for EEG Emotion
    Recognition
    Liu, Chao, Ma, Sha, Sun, Li, Chang (China Medical University)
    SUMMARY: A hybrid CNN-transformer with a learnable Gaussian-like
    convolution kernel for noise filtering, reporting 74.23%+-2.59% on
    DEAP and 67.17%+-1.70% on SEED-V, with band-importance analysis
    finding beta/gamma bands most influential.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: architecturally tangential; its interpretability
    angle (learned-kernel/band analysis) is a different specific technique
    from this project's own SHAP diagnostic (#13), not adopted directly.

25. LEL: Lipschitz Continuity Constrained Ensemble Learning for Efficient
    EEG-Based Intra-Subject Emotion Recognition
    Gong, Li, Kang, Chai, Zeng, Yan, Zhang, Siok, Wang (Shanghai Maritime
    University / Hong Kong Polytechnic University et al.)
    SUMMARY: Enforces Lipschitz-continuity constraints on transformer
    attention/spectral-extraction/normalization modules plus a learnable
    ensemble fusion strategy; evaluated on EAV, FACED, and SEED -- NOT
    DEAP -- for INTRA-subject (not subject-independent) emotion
    recognition.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED, and the least directly relevant paper in the corpus
    to this project: it targets a different dataset set, a different
    (intra-subject, not subject-independent) evaluation regime, and a
    different architecture family. Included in the review corpus for
    general ensemble/stability-technique awareness only.

26. EEG Emotion Recognition Through Deep Learning
    Dolgopolyi, Chatzipanagiotou (American College of Greece)
    SUMMARY: A CNN-Transformer architecture for 3-class (positive/neutral/
    negative) emotion classification trained on a custom merged SEED/
    SEED-FRA/SEED-GER dataset, achieving 91% test accuracy while using
    only 5 of 62 electrodes.
    NOT IMPLEMENTED: not DEAP-based; reviewed for its channel-reduction
    result (relevant background for practical/consumer-EEG deployment
    discussions) but not adopted.

RECOMMENDATION
--------------
Before this project is written up as a formal thesis/paper, decide whether
to (a) keep the full 32-paper corpus and cite Part B's entries as general
background/related-work, correctly labeled as such, or (b) trim
papers_EEG/ down to the 13 papers in Part A that actually drove an
implemented experiment, to keep the bibliography tight. Either is
defensible; what would NOT be defensible is citing any Part B paper as if
it directly informed a specific implemented technique, since -- based on
this reconstruction -- none of them did.
