CITATIONS — related/concurrent work for the DEAP subject-independent evaluation study
========================================================================================
Compiled 2026-08-27. These three papers were found via a web-literature check on the
novelty of this notebook's central finding (accuracy on DEAP looks strong under naive
splits but collapses to near-chance AUC/balanced-accuracy once genuine subject-independent
evaluation is enforced). They are all 2026 publications, contemporaneous with this work,
and should be cited in the paper as independent corroboration rather than treated as prior
art this work failed to notice.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[1] Pandilova, E., Stojmenski, A., Chorbev, I., Petrov, M., Kitanovski, I., & Trajanov, D.
    "Subject Identity Confounds qEEG Emotion Recognition on DEAP and DREAMER."
    Sensors, 26(17), 5327 (2026). https://doi.org/10.3390/s26175327
    https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/17/5327

    Why added: This is the closest and sharpest concurrent match to this notebook's
    core finding. On DEAP, they show epoch-pooled (leaky) evaluation gives ROC-AUC
    0.689 (valence) / 0.711 (arousal), which collapses to 0.493 / 0.447 under
    participant-independent evaluation — i.e., AUC at chance, matching this notebook's
    ~0.45-0.56 range under GroupKFold. They go further than this notebook: the same
    qEEG features identify *subject identity* with 0.998 accuracy on DEAP, and a
    predictor using NO EEG at all (just each subject's training-set positive rate)
    explains 42-84% of the "above-chance" discrimination models get credit for.
    Cite this to (a) corroborate the near-chance AUC result with an independent
    pipeline/lab, and (b) motivate why GroupKFold/subject-grouping alone is necessary
    but not sufficient — subject-identity leakage can survive even grouped splits via
    the features themselves. Directly relevant to Track A's DE/Hjorth/FAA/connectivity
    features (same feature families they re-evaluate).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[2] Sadegh-Zadeh, S.-A., Saadat, S., Sadeghimanesh, Z., Soleymani, O., Movahhedi, T.,
    Jarchi, D., & Saadat, M.
    "Standard Accuracy in EEG Emotion Recognition Is Predictable from Class
    Distribution Alone: A Systematic Analysis of Labelling Threshold Effects in the
    DEAP Dataset."
    Frontiers in Neuroinformatics, 20, Article 1830324 (2026).
    https://doi.org/10.3389/fninf.2026.1830324

    Why added: Near-identical scope to this notebook's Section 14 (label-cleanup
    ablation), published as a standalone paper. They sweep the DEAP binarization
    threshold (4.0-6.0) on valence/arousal under within-subject vs. cross-subject
    (LOSO) protocols and show standard accuracy tracks the majority-class baseline
    while balanced accuracy sits at/near chance (exactly 50% cross-subject at
    thresholds >=4.5); threshold choice alone accounts for 14-23 percentage points of
    reported accuracy on identical data. Cite this to support this notebook's
    class-imbalance/labeling-threshold explanation for inflated accuracy (Sections 4,
    13, 14) with an independent, threshold-focused replication, and to frame the
    per_subject_z / drop_middle label variants here as extending their single-threshold
    sweep to alternative label-construction strategies.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[3] Suo, H. & Li, Y.
    "Evaluation Protocols and Cross-Subject Generalization in EEG Emotion Recognition."
    arXiv:2607.27655 (submitted 2026-07-30). https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27655

    Why added: The field-level, protocol-focused version of this notebook's argument,
    applied to SEED/SEED-IV rather than DEAP. They show training accuracy >0.99 vs.
    held-out-participant accuracy of ~0.53 (SEED) / ~0.40 (SEED-IV), and propose a
    three-layer reporting framework (target estimand / development procedure /
    reporting specification) so subject-dependent, subject-disjoint, and cross-session
    results aren't conflated. Cite this to (a) show the evaluation-protocol critique
    generalizes beyond DEAP to other major EEG-emotion corpora (SEED), reinforcing that
    this is a dataset-agnostic methodological issue, not a DEAP-specific quirk, and
    (b) borrow their reporting recommendations (explicit target-data access, training-fit
    diagnostics, pre-specified final evaluation) for how this notebook's own results
    tables should be framed in the paper.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
How these three fit together for the paper's related-work / discussion section:
  - [1] and [2] are DEAP-specific and directly corroborate the near-chance AUC finding
    from two different angles (subject-identity leakage vs. label-threshold artifact).
  - [3] shows the same failure mode holds on SEED, generalizing the critique beyond DEAP.
  - None of the three do the specific things this notebook does: (a) directly rebut a
    named, recently published paper's reported numbers (Hasan & Islam, MethodsX 2025,
    main.pdf) on matched features/classifiers, (b) cover all four DEAP label dimensions
    (valence, arousal, dominance, liking) rather than just valence/arousal, or (c) run a
    controlled head-to-head of 6 classical (DE+Hjorth+FAA+connectivity) vs. 4 deep-learning
    (raw-window) models under one identical subject-independent protocol.
  - Recommended framing: position this work as convergent, independent confirmation
    (three groups reaching the same conclusion in 2026 strengthens credibility) plus a
    named-paper rebuttal and broader dimension/model coverage as the added contribution.
